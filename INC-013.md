# INC-013 — SharePoint Online Document Read-Only Access

**Type:** Simulated service desk incident  
**Service:** Microsoft SharePoint Online / Microsoft 365  
**Priority:** P3 (training scenario)  
**Status:** Resolved  
**Affected user:** Rachel Brooks (`rbrooks@adensvaygmail.onmicrosoft.com`)  
**Site:** `https://adensvaygmail.sharepoint.com/sites/testing`  
**Document:** `Q4-Project-Plan.docx`

## Incident summary

Rachel reported that she could open the `testing` SharePoint site and the Q4 project plan document, but could no longer edit the document. She indicated that editing had worked previously.

## Environment

- Hybrid Active Directory / Microsoft Entra ID training tenant
- Microsoft 365 Business Basic trial
- SharePoint Online team site associated with the `testing` Microsoft 365 group
- Separate test identities for John Smith (document owner) and Rachel Brooks (affected user)

## Symptoms and impact

- SharePoint site remained accessible to Rachel.
- The affected document opened successfully but was read-only for Rachel.
- Rachel could not make and save the required updates.
- Scope was narrowed to document editing rather than a complete sign-in or site-access failure.

## Investigation

1. Reproduced the read-only behavior while signed in as Rachel using a separate browser session.
2. Reviewed the site's membership in SharePoint Admin Center.
3. Confirmed Rachel and John were members of the `testing` Microsoft 365 group.
4. Distinguished site-level group membership from item-level permissions.
5. Reviewed the document's unique permissions in the lab. The fault injection had changed Rachel's direct permission from **Contribute** to **Read** and removed the **testing Members** group's **Edit** permission from this document.
6. Attempted to inspect the site using the `entraadmin` administrative identity. Tenant-level SharePoint administration did not automatically provide document access. The administrative people picker failed, and a SharePoint Online PowerShell module-loading issue was encountered; these tooling issues were deferred rather than treated as the root cause of Rachel's incident.

## Root cause

**Simulated document-level permission misconfiguration.** The document had unique permissions. Rachel retained Read access but lacked an effective permission granting editing. Membership in the site's Microsoft 365 group did not, by itself, guarantee Edit access to this uniquely permissioned document.

## Resolution

In the controlled homelab, signed in as the test document owner, John Smith, and changed Rachel's direct document permission from **Read** to **Contribute** through **Manage access → Advanced permissions**.

This was a targeted fix for Rachel. The `testing Members` group's document-level Edit permission was **not** restored as part of the incident fix; restoring that group permission would potentially affect additional users. The lab's exact original permissions can be restored separately during environment reset.

## Validation

- Rachel successfully regained access to edit the document.
- Site access remained available.
- No tenant-wide or site-wide permission change was required to resolve Rachel's individual editing issue.

## Production workflow considerations

In a real organization, a technician would confirm the business requirement for editing access with the document or site owner, use an authorized support identity or guide the owner through the change, and follow applicable identity verification, approval, and auditing procedures. An L1 technician might escalate the permission change rather than self-assign privileged access. Users' passwords should not be requested or used by support staff.

## Lessons learned

- Separate site membership from effective document permissions.
- A SharePoint document with unique permissions may not grant the same access as its parent site or library.
- A user's effective access may derive from multiple direct and group grants; changing one grant may not be sufficient.
- Prefer the smallest authorized permission change that resolves the incident.
- Avoid escalating a routine document-sharing issue into unnecessary tenant-administration troubleshooting.
- Timebox tooling problems that are not essential to resolving the user's incident.

## Suggested ServiceNow closure notes

**Investigation:** Verified site access and Microsoft 365 group membership; identified restrictive unique document permissions.  
**Resolution:** Restored the user's document-level Contribute permission through the authorized test document owner.  
**Verification:** User confirmed editing access was restored.  
**Status:** Resolved.
